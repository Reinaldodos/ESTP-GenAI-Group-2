# Accessibility and Medical Travel Needs

Employees may request reasonable travel arrangements for a disability, medical condition, temporary injury, or other accessibility requirement. Requests should be made early enough to arrange suitable transportation, accommodation, or assistance.

Detailed medical information must be shared only through the designated confidential process. A manager needs to know the approved travel arrangement and its business impact but does not need a diagnosis. Documents containing medical details must not be attached to a general trip request or expense claim.

Approved accommodations may include an accessible hotel room, step-free transport, airport or station assistance, an appropriate seat, additional baggage for medical equipment, or travel at a time that reduces a documented health risk. Necessary approved costs are business expenses and are not treated as personal upgrades.

An extra-legroom seat may be booked without the usual manager exception when it is part of an approved medical or accessibility accommodation. Business class is not automatically authorized by an accessibility request; if business class is needed, it must be specifically approved or allowed by the standard rule for a flight segment longer than six hours.

A support person may travel when the designated review process confirms that assistance is necessary. The company may then cover the support person's reasonable transportation and accommodation under the approved arrangement. Personal companions who are not approved support persons remain the employee's responsibility.

Necessary service-animal fees and documentation costs may be reimbursable. Ordinary pet travel is personal and is not reimbursable. The employee should work with the travel contact because carrier and destination requirements can differ.

Travelers are responsible for routine medication and for checking destination restrictions. Refrigeration or secure storage may be requested when needed. Medication itself is normally a personal healthcare expense.

If an unexpected accessibility or medical need arises during travel, the employee should prioritize health and safety and contact travel assistance. Necessary immediate changes may be made before written approval when delay would create a reasonable risk, with documentation provided afterward.
