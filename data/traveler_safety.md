# Traveler Safety and Emergencies

Employees should review destination risks, weather, transport conditions, and security notices. Trip registration must include a reliable contact method, and assistance details should be kept outside checked luggage.

Personal safety takes priority over cost or schedule. An employee may leave an unsafe location, use a taxi instead of public transportation, or arrange temporary accommodation without prior approval when waiting would create a reasonable risk. The employee should contact the travel assistance service and manager as soon as it is safe.

For an immediate threat to life or health, the traveler should contact local emergency services first. The company travel assistance contact can then help coordinate medical support, safe transportation, replacement travel, or communication with the employee's manager and family contact.

Reasonable emergency expenses connected to the trip are reimbursable. The traveler should retain receipts when possible and record the event and why normal approval was unavailable. A missing receipt does not automatically prevent review.

Employees who become ill should seek appropriate medical care and notify their manager if the illness affects the itinerary or work commitments. Routine personal medication is the traveler's responsibility. Medical costs may be handled through applicable insurance or assistance arrangements and should not be assumed to follow the ordinary meal or transport reimbursement process.

A lost or stolen passport must be reported to local authorities when required, to the relevant consular service, and to the travel assistance contact. A lost corporate card must be reported immediately to the card issuer and finance. Theft of company equipment must also be reported through the company security process.

Employees must not drive when impaired, dangerously tired, or unfamiliar conditions make driving unsafe. They should use seat belts and licensed transport providers and should follow local law. A manager must not require an employee to continue a journey that the employee reasonably considers unsafe.

After a serious incident, the traveler should complete the relevant safety report when able. The report is separate from the expense claim: the safety report records the event, while the expense claim documents the resulting business costs.
