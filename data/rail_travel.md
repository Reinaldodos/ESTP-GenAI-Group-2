# Rail Travel

Rail should be considered when it is a practical alternative to flying or driving. Employees should compare terminal access, transfers, and productive travel time. The lowest reasonable fare meeting the business schedule should be selected.

Standard class is the normal class of rail travel. First class may be booked when a single scheduled rail journey lasts more than four hours and the employee needs to work confidentially, or when first class costs no more than EUR 40 above the available flexible standard fare. Manager approval is required before purchasing first class.

Flexible tickets may be selected when a meeting time is genuinely uncertain or when a restricted ticket would create a significant risk of buying a replacement. The traveler should record the business reason for choosing flexibility. Convenience alone is not sufficient when a suitable fixed train is available.

Seat reservations and mandatory booking fees are reimbursable. A quiet-zone reservation may be selected at no additional cost. Meals, station lounge access, entertainment, and optional priority services follow the normal expense rules and are not automatically covered by the rail ticket policy.

For a same-day journey, public transportation should normally be used to reach the station. A taxi may be used when public transportation is unavailable, unsafe, or likely to cause a significant delay. Parking at a station is reimbursable when its total cost is lower than reasonable local transportation for the same trip.

Travelers should use an approved booking channel when the required route is available there. If a local operator cannot be booked through that channel, the employee may buy directly and submit the ticket receipt with the expense claim. The receipt must show the route, date, class, and amount paid.

Disruption costs caused by a cancelled or severely delayed train may be claimed when they are necessary and reasonable. The traveler should first use any rebooking, accommodation, or meal assistance offered by the operator and should retain evidence of the disruption.

Rail tickets purchased for a business trip must not be transferred to another person. Refunds and credits belong to the company when the original ticket was company-funded, and cancellations should be made promptly when travel is no longer required.
