# International Travel Preparation

International trips must be registered with the travel department at least five working days before departure. Registration includes the itinerary, accommodation, emergency contact method, and personal dates. It is required even when another employee booked.

The traveler is responsible for holding a valid passport, visa, work authorization, vaccination evidence, or other document required for the destination and planned activity. The travel department may provide guidance, but that guidance does not transfer responsibility from the traveler.

Reasonable visa fees and mandatory entry charges for an approved business trip are reimbursable. Optional expedited processing requires manager approval unless standard processing could not meet the approved travel dates for reasons outside the employee's control. Passport issuance and renewal are personal expenses unless a passport is required solely because of an approved business assignment.

Employees should review entry and security information and complete any required higher-risk briefing. A trip may be postponed or cancelled when risks cannot be reduced to an acceptable level.

Business equipment, samples, and technical material may be subject to customs or export controls. The employee must contact the appropriate internal specialist before carrying controlled items across a border. Travelers must not describe business goods as personal property to avoid a declaration or fee.

Necessary roaming, local SIM, or mobile data costs may be claimed when they support business communication and a reasonable option is selected. Premium entertainment packages and personal calls are not reimbursable. Employees should follow company security guidance when accessing business systems abroad.

Medical treatment requirements and accessibility needs should be raised early enough to arrange reasonable support. Private medical details should be shared only through the designated confidential process, not included in a general trip request or expense claim.

If an international itinerary changes during travel, the employee should update the travel department as soon as practical. In an emergency, immediate safety takes priority, but the traveler or a colleague should provide the revised location and contact details once it is safe to do so.
