# Ground Transportation

Employees should use practical public transportation. Operating hours, luggage, safety, accessibility, and arrival time should be considered. The cheapest option is not required if it creates a significant delay or safety concern.

Taxis and licensed ride services may be used when public transportation is unavailable, unsafe, inaccessible, or likely to cause a significant delay. They may also be appropriate for transporting heavy business equipment or for a shared journey when the total fare is lower than separate public transportation costs.

A taxi or ride-service claim should include a receipt and the business origin and destination. Premium vehicles are not reimbursable unless a larger vehicle is needed for a group, luggage, equipment, or accessibility.

Rental cars require manager approval before booking. A rental may be justified when several business locations must be visited, when public transportation is impractical, or when the total rental and fuel cost is lower than other reasonable transport. Employees should book a compact or standard vehicle suitable for the number of travelers.

Rental charges, necessary fuel, tolls, and business parking are reimbursable. Upgrades, fines, penalties, personal fuel, and sightseeing mileage are not. Duplicate optional insurance requires a documented reason.

Employees using a personal vehicle for an approved business journey may claim the company mileage rate published by finance. The mileage allowance covers fuel, routine maintenance, depreciation, and personal insurance. Tolls and necessary business parking may be claimed separately, but fuel may not be claimed in addition to mileage.

Commuting between an employee's home and normal workplace is a personal expense. Travel from the normal workplace to a temporary business location, or between temporary business locations, may be reimbursable. When a trip begins at home, only mileage above the employee's normal commute may be claimed unless the journey is to an airport or station for an approved business trip.

Travelers must follow local law, wear seat belts, and never drive when impaired or dangerously tired. In unsafe conditions, they should stop and seek assistance.
