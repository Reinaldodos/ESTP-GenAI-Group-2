# Sustainable Business Travel

Employees should consider whether a remote meeting can achieve the objective before requesting travel. Necessary in-person work remains permitted, with value considered alongside cost, time, and environmental impact.

Rail should be considered instead of air travel when the complete rail journey is practical and does not prevent the employee from meeting the business schedule. The comparison should include access to terminals, transfers, and productive travel time rather than ticket duration alone.

For local travel, walking, cycling, and public transportation are encouraged when they are safe, accessible, and practical. Sustainability does not override safety or accessibility. A taxi or licensed ride service remains appropriate when public transportation is unavailable, unsafe, inaccessible, or would cause a significant delay.

When several employees attend the same event, they should coordinate schedules where practical, share ground transportation, and avoid unnecessary duplicate rentals. Sharing is not required when it would create an unreasonable delay, compromise safety, or conflict with an approved accessibility arrangement.

Travelers should prefer a suitable company-negotiated hotel or another property near the business location when this reduces unnecessary local transport. The normal EUR 180 nightly limit and approval requirements continue to apply; a sustainability preference does not authorize a higher room rate by itself.

Employees should select electronic tickets and receipts when accepted and should avoid printing documents that can be used securely in digital form. They should follow company information-security requirements and retain the evidence needed for an expense claim.

Voluntary carbon offsets, environmental donations, and personal membership fees are not reimbursable unless they are purchased through a specific company program. Employees must not add an optional offset to a personal expense claim simply because the underlying trip was approved.

Sustainability considerations should be documented briefly when they materially affect the chosen itinerary, but employees are not expected to calculate emissions for every trip. Policy limits, business necessity, personal safety, accessibility, and reasonable journey time remain part of the final decision.
